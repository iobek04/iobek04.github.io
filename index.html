Ipek / the short version

I'm curious about how we learn, explore, and make decisions.

Broadly, I'm interested in how our experiences and the statistics of our environments influence how we learn and explore.

Right now, I'm focusing on how we adapt our learning processes to the demands of varied contexts, especially when those contexts involve cognitive trade-offs, such as deciding when to persist and when to be flexible.

Ipek Naz / what I'm doing now

I'm the first lab manager and a research technician in Kate Nussenbaum's Computation, Learning, and Development Lab at Boston University.

The lab studies how value-guided learning, memory, and exploration change from childhood to early adulthood: how we flexibly adapt our learning processes to the demands of different contexts, how we learn the structure of our environment and use it to guide later learning and decisions, and how we learn to learn over multiple timescales of experience.

You can explore the projects I'm working on in the Research tab.

Naz / my time at Penn

I studied Cognitive Science, Computational Neuroscience, and Data Science at UPenn, where I was a Benjamin Franklin Scholar and a MindCORE Scholar.

For my senior honors thesis, advised by Sharon Thompson-Schill, Alison Gopnik, and Annya Dahmani, I studied how people infer how much someone has learned just by watching the choices they make.

In a game where a player hops across logs to cross a river, participants saw which level a player started on and which level they chose next, then judged how much of the first level the player had completed. I compared these human judgments with GPT-4o's, in settings where the player either had a goal or was exploring freely.
