Hi, I'm Ipek. I'm interested in building models of thinking.

I'm currently a predoctoral researcher and lab manager at Boston University’s Computation, Learning, and Development Lab. I recently graduated from the University of Pennsylvania, where I studied Cognitive Science with a concentration in Computation and Cognition and completed minors in Data Science and Computational Neuroscience.

I am broadly interested in how people learn from experience, make decisions, and form judgments, as well as how these processes can be formalized through computational models of the mind. I am especially interested in learning progress, exploration, curiosity, value-guided learning, and the relationship between human learning and artificial intelligence.

Previously, I worked with Alison Gopnik and Annya Dahmani at the Cognitive Development and Learning Lab at UC Berkeley. There, I studied how children and adults evaluate their own learning progress and decide what to explore next. My senior honors thesis compared human judgments about learning progress with computational learning models and examined how goals shape exploration. You can watch my senior thesis presentation .

I also worked at Penn’s Center for Neuroengineering and Therapeutics, where I used neuroimaging and computational tools to study the relationship between traumatic brain injury lesions and epilepsy.

Interactive Demo

Submarine Fishing Task

A value-based foraging task: on each trial, people choose among creatures worth different (and changing) amounts, studying how people track and search for value under uncertainty across high- and low-volatility conditions.

Web-Surf Task

A sunk-cost decision-making task: people decide whether to wait for content to load or cut their losses and move on, studying how prior time investment shapes the decision to persist versus abandon.
