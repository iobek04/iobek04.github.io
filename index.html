Ipek / the short version

I'm interested in building models of thinking.

I study how people learn from experience, make decisions, and form judgments, and how those processes can be written down as computational models of the mind. The questions I keep coming back to are about learning progress, exploration, curiosity, value-guided learning, and what human learning and artificial intelligence can teach each other.

Ipek Naz / what I'm doing now

I'm a predoctoral researcher and lab manager at the Computation, Learning, and Development Lab, where I build tasks that you can try yourself.

In the Submarine Fishing Task, people choose among sea creatures whose values keep changing, which lets us study how people track and search for value under uncertainty in high- and low-volatility environments. The task is taken from Holton et al. (2024), and this project is a collaboration with Eleanor Holton.

In the Web-Surf Task, people decide whether to wait for content to load or cut their losses and move on, which lets us study how time already invested shapes the decision to persist or abandon.

There's more on these, plus a learning progress study in the works, on my research page.

Naz / where I came from

I studied Computation and Cognition, Data Science, and Computational Neuroscience at UPenn, where I was also a MindCORE Scholar.

I worked with Alison Gopnik and Annya Dahmani at the Cognitive Development and Learning Lab at UC Berkeley, studying how children and adults evaluate their own learning progress and decide what to explore next. My senior honors thesis, co-advised by Sharon Thompson-Schill, compared human judgments about learning progress with artificial learning models and examined how goals shape exploration. You can watch my senior thesis presentation.
